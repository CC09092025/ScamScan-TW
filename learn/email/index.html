Learn — Email

In 1971, Ray Tomlinson, a computer engineer at BBN Technologies, modified message-sending programs for the ARPANET network to send messages between different computers, introducing the @ symbol to route messages to a specific host computer (user@host).

Today, while less common for casual online conversations, email remains widely used for professional communication, business correspondence, and formal inquiries. This gives email a high level of perceived trustworthiness, making it an attractive medium for scammers to deceive victims and exploit that trust.

While most popular email providers can now detect and filter scam and phishing emails, they aren't always reliable. Just as legitimate emails can be incorrectly flagged as spam, malicious emails can slip through these filters and reach your inbox. Therefore, it is important to know how to identify illegitimate emails without relying solely on your email provider to get it right.

01

Check the sender

Always check who sent you an email before trusting its contents. Scammers often impersonate legitimate companies, organizations, or individuals by using display names and email addresses that look convincing at first glance. Pay close attention to the actual email address, particularly the domain name after the @ symbol. A familiar display name does not necessarily mean the email came from a legitimate sender.

Examples

support@google.com Legitimate
support@googIe.com Suspicious

The lowercase "l" in Google has been replaced with an uppercase "I", which can look similar in certain fonts.

google-support@gmail.com Suspicious

While gmail.com is a legitimate email provider, it would be unusual for a company like Google to use a generic email provider instead of its official domain.

support@google.security-check.com Suspicious

Although it may appear legitimate, the actual registered domain is security-check.com, not google.com.

Recommendation

Refer to our tips for identifying suspicious URLs to help determine whether an email sender's address is legitimate.

Quick Tip

Although scammers may use deceptive domains to impersonate legitimate businesses, they sometimes rely on victims assuming an email is legitimate simply because it uses a familiar display name. Instead, they may use random or unrelated email domains, which are often cheaper to obtain. If the domain following the @ symbol looks unfamiliar or unrelated to the supposed sender, treat the email with suspicion.

02

Be cautious of unexpected attachments

Unexpected email attachments can contain malicious files designed to infect your device, steal information, or install unwanted software. Be especially cautious of unfamiliar file types, executable files, and documents that ask you to enable macros. If you weren't expecting an attachment, verify with the sender through another communication method before opening it.

Examples

Invoice_93821.pdf.exe Suspicious

An executable file disguised as a PDF invoice. The file extension is always the part of the filename that appears to the right of the final .. Any extensions appearing before it are simply part of the filename.

Resume_Carter.docm Suspicious

An unexpected Word document that may contain malicious macros.

Package_Tracking.zip Suspicious

An unexpected compressed attachment claiming to contain delivery information.

Payment_Receipt.html Suspicious

An HTML attachment that could redirect you to a fraudulent login page.

Quick Tip

On Windows and macOS, displaying file extensions may be disabled by default. To make sure you can see the complete filename and its extension, follow the instructions for your operating system below.

Windows
  1. Open File Explorer.
  2. Access the folder options:
    • Windows 11: Click the three dots (...) on the toolbar and select Options.
    • Windows 10: Click File in the top-left corner, then select Change folder and search options.
  3. Switch to the View tab.
  4. Uncheck Hide extensions for known file types.
  5. Click Apply, then OK.
macOS
  1. Open a Finder window.
  2. Click Finder in the menu bar and select Settings (or Preferences on older versions).
  3. Open the Advanced tab.
  4. Enable Show all filename extensions.
Linux

Most Linux file managers provide an option to display hidden file extensions. Alternatively, you can use the file command in the terminal to identify a file's actual type.

file suspicious-file.pdf
03

Watch out for urgency and threats

Scammers often create a false sense of urgency to pressure you into acting without thinking. Messages may claim that your account will be suspended, a payment is overdue, or you've won a prize that requires immediate action. Be suspicious of emails that pressure you into clicking links, providing information, or making payments.

Examples of Urgency and Threats

Account Suspension

"Your Microsoft account will be permanently suspended within 24 hours unless you verify your identity."

Unpaid Invoice

"Your payment of $499 is overdue. Pay immediately to avoid additional charges."

Security Alert

"We detected suspicious activity on your bank account. Click here immediately to prevent unauthorized access."

Prize Notification

"Congratulations! You've been selected to receive a $1,000 gift card. Claim your prize within the next 30 minutes."

Government Threat

"An outstanding tax balance has been detected. Failure to pay immediately may result in legal action."

Quick Tip

If you find yourself worrying about an urgent threat or demand in an email, take a moment to step back and assess the situation. Don't let the sender pressure you into acting immediately. Instead, independently visit the organization's official website or contact them through a verified communication channel to confirm whether the situation is legitimate.

04

Check for suspicious links

Emails can contain hyperlinks that disguise their actual destinations. Before clicking anything, hover over links on desktop or press and hold them on mobile to preview the destination. Make sure the URL matches the organization the email claims to represent.

Recommendation

Refer to our tips for identifying suspicious URLs to help determine whether a hyperlink is legitimate.

05

Never share sensitive information

Legitimate organizations generally won't ask you to send passwords, verification codes, or other sensitive information through email. Be particularly cautious of messages requesting banking information, account credentials, or personal identification.

Examples of Requests for Sensitive Information

Password Request

"Reply to this email with your account password to verify your identity."

Verification Code Request

"Please forward the six-digit verification code we just sent you to confirm your account."

Banking Information

"Your direct deposit information is outdated. Reply with your account and transit numbers to avoid payment delays."

Identity Verification

"Please attach a photo of your driver's licence and other identification documents to complete your verification."

Quick Tip

Never send passwords or verification codes through email, even if the sender appears legitimate. If an organization needs to verify your identity, use its official website or application.

06

Watch out for unexpected requests

Be cautious of emails requesting unusual actions, especially when they involve money, gift cards, payments, or changes to account information. Scammers may impersonate coworkers, managers, or family members to make their requests appear legitimate. Verify unusual requests through another communication method.

Examples of Requests for Sensitive Information

Gift Cards

"I need you to purchase five $100 gift cards and send me the activation codes. I'll reimburse you later." (Impersonating a manager.)

Urgent Payment

"Please transfer $4,500 to this new bank account before the end of the day." (Impersonating a business executive.)

Payroll Changes

"We've updated our banking provider. Please change your direct deposit information using the attached form."

Family Emergency

"Hey, it's me. I lost my phone and need you to send me some money. I'll explain everything later."

Quick Tip

If someone requests money or an unusual action, contact them through a separate, previously established communication method. Don't rely on the contact information provided in the suspicious email.

07

Look for inconsistencies

Pay attention to unusual wording, spelling mistakes, inconsistent branding, and formatting issues. While these aren't definitive signs of a scam, they can indicate that an email isn't from the organization it claims to represent.

Examples of Inconsistencies

Spelling Mistakes

An email from a supposed professional organization contains numerous spelling and grammatical errors.

Inconsistent Branding

An email claiming to be from Microsoft uses outdated logos, incorrect colours, or unfamiliar formatting.

Unusual Wording

A supposedly Canadian bank sends an email containing awkward phrasing or terminology that doesn't match its usual communications.

Poor Formatting

Text is misaligned, images appear broken, or different fonts and formatting styles are used throughout the message.

Quick Tip

Compare suspicious emails with previous messages from the same organization. However, remember that well-written emails with convincing branding can still be fraudulent.

08

Be careful with reply-to addresses

Always check who sent you an email before trusting its contents. Scammers often impersonate legitimate companies, organizations, or individuals by using display names and email addresses that look convincing at first glance. Pay close attention to the actual email address, particularly the domain name after the @ symbol. A familiar display name does not necessarily mean the email came from a legitimate sender.

Examples

Sender Legitimate
support@google.com Reply-To support@google.com
Sender support@google.com Reply-To
support@google-support.com Suspicious

Different domains.

Sender support@google.com Reply-To
support.google@gmail.com Suspicious

Uses a personal email provider.

Sender support@google.com Reply-To
johndoe123@outlook.com Suspicious

Unrelated email address.

Quick Tip

Most email clients allow you to inspect the sender's details or view the original message headers. If the reply-to address is different or unexpected, independently verify it before responding.

Note

A different Reply-To address isn't automatically malicious. Organizations sometimes use third-party services to handle responses. What matters is whether the destination is expected and can be verified.

Additional Recommendations

If you're still unsure whether an email is legitimate, there are a couple ways to verify it. First, vistit the compan'ys official website and look for its listed contact information. Alternatively, find a previous email from the same company and compare the sender's email address with the one you're suspicious of. Second, if the company provides a support address, contact them directly and ask whether the message is legitimate. You can include a screenshot of the suspicious email, making sure to censor any sensitive or personal information. Their support team should be able to help you determine whether the email is genuine.
